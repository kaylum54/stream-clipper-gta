# Monetising stream clips

Checked 18 Nov 2026. This is research, not legal or financial advice. Nothing here is an income promise.

## Summary

The one route with clear, repeated evidence of per-view payouts to clippers is a paid clipping campaign: a streamer, creator or brand sets a rate per 1,000 views and a budget, and clippers post clips from their own accounts. Whop Content Rewards and Vyro are the two marketplaces I could confirm. Rates seen in sources run from about $1 to $3 per 1,000 views. Big streamers (N3on, MrBeast) pay about $40 to $50 per 100,000 views, which is $0.40 to $0.50 per 1,000, through their own programmes.

Platform creator funds are a weak fit. YouTube's reused content policy, TikTok's originality language, Meta's March 2026 originality rules and X's Original Content Rewards all aim at original work. A clip of someone else's stream may be ineligible, even with the streamer's permission (YouTube says so directly). Kick does not publish a clipper programme or rate. Several figures online are second-hand, and I have marked them.

## 1. Paid campaigns

### Whop Content Rewards

- Owner sets a reward rate per 1,000 views, a total budget, an optional minimum payout (views before a clip enters review), a maximum per video and an optional flat fee. The owner approves or rejects. Approved submissions are paid automatically by views. (Whop docs.)
- The docs page does not give platform fees, holds or how views are checked. The terms say Whop counts only views it judges legitimate, excludes bots, and may withhold or claw back. The terms charge the seller a 10% fee on amounts paid to participants. They state no minimum payout and no guaranteed earnings. Participants must be 18 or older, must disclose the relationship clearly beside the clip, and may not take payment off-platform. (Whop terms, undated.)
- A newer set of Creator Terms under Content Rewards Inc was shown in a search snippet (7-day earning period then 3-day hold; 10% creator fee). I did not open it. Treat as unverified.
- Rates on live campaigns: the official pages I opened show none. Third-party sources: Clyper (checked 23 Oct to 16 Nov 2026) lists IShowSpeed at $1 per 1,000 views ($10 to $500 a clip), and several streamers (Kai Cenat, Clix, Marlon and others) paying a share of a fixed pool split by views. A median of about $1.09 per 1,000 from a 10 Nov snapshot of 707 campaigns came only from a search summary of a page I could not open (403). Not verified.
- Many campaigns say the clips must come from the campaign's source content and carry set captions or tags. Read each campaign's rules.

### Vyro

- MrBeast's platform. Creators and brands post briefs, clippers post clips and are paid by views. One campaign paid $3 per 1,000 views. Payouts hourly to crypto, PayPal or bank (Tubefilter, 14 Oct 2025).
- Clyper lists current rounds at $1 to $2.50 per 1,000 views with caps up to $1,000 a clip.
- Fee and review-window claims in blogs conflict (9%, 10%, 72 hours, 30 days). I found no official source. Not verified.

### Others

- Clipping.net: referred to in search results as a separate marketplace with per-campaign minimum views (10K, 100K). I could not open the site (429). Not verified here.
- ClipFarm (Airrack, launched Aug 2025) is named by TheWrap (27 Mar 2026). I did not find its rates or terms. Not verified.
- Clipping (agency): see route 5.

## 2. Streamers' own programmes

Public sources show:
- N3on: reported $40 per 100,000 views (sometimes $50); about $1.4 million to 303 clippers in five weeks; he says he pays them (Complex 23 Apr 2026, citing documents given to Business Insider). Access is not public.
- MrBeast: $50 per 100,000 views reported by Bloomberg Law and Complex (Oct to Nov 2025); runs Vyro.
- Clyper's table (checked 23 Oct to 16 Nov 2026) lists several others on Content Rewards or Vyro. Campaigns open and close, so check each listing.

Join through the streamer's listing on the platform they name. Do not pay anyone for access.

## 3. Kick

- win.gg (12 Nov 2025) says the Kick Clipping Programme is official, streamers apply in the Kick Discord, and it names no rate.
- Clavicular said on a podcast that Kick pays for clipping and spent nearly $700,000 in a month (Dexerto, 30 Jul 2026). Kick had not confirmed it.
- Tubefilter (29 Apr 2026) says about half of N3on's network is "apparently paid by Kick". N3on himself says he pays the clippers and claims Kick pays some others (Complex).
- FindClout says Kick has not published a per-view or per-clip rate. Search results said Bloomberg reported in April 2026 that Kick's campaigns run through Clipping. I did not open Bloomberg.
- Verdict: no public Kick clipper rate or sign-up for individual clippers that I could verify. Keep it out of any promise to users.

## 4. Platform creator funds

Common point: these programmes are designed around original work. Account-level eligibility figures below are from the platform source named; payout rates are not published by any of them.

**TikTok Creator Rewards.** The US terms (updated 20 Jul 2026) require 18+, 10,000 authentic followers, 100,000 authentic views in the last 30 days, a personal account in good standing, and videos "at least 1 minute" long. Government, political, business and organisation accounts are not eligible. The terms say you are responsible for ownership and originality but do not define original content. TikTok's 2024 newsroom post says videos must be "over one minute" and rewards "original content". The two wordings differ, so a clip of exactly 60 seconds is a risk. Regions: an older Creator Academy snippet lists the US, UK, Germany, Japan, South Korea, France, Mexico and Brazil. I could not open the current list (page returned no content). Other terms (Japan/Korea) use 50,000 followers.

**YouTube Partner Programme (Shorts).** YouTube Help today: 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. The Help page says updates apply from 1 Feb 2027 and you must accept new terms by 31 Jan 2027. Tubefilter (10 Aug 2026): from 1 Feb 2027, new applicants need 8,000 watch hours or 20 million Shorts views; Shorts ad earnings need 10 million Shorts views in 90 days for everyone; current partners keep their status. I did not find YouTube's Shorts revenue split. Reused content policy (YouTube Help): "clips of moments... edited together with little or no narrative" and content copied without substantive change are ineligible. "This policy applies even if you have permission from the original creator." Eligible examples include commentary, critical review and a storyline added.

**Facebook Content Monetization.** Invite-only, with an interest form in the Professional Dashboard (Meta pages, 2024 to 2026). Only public content on Pages, professional-mode profiles, Events and Groups qualifies. Meta's 13 Mar 2026 post says re-uploads and minor changes such as borders, captions or speed are unoriginal, and clips count as original only with substantial creative value. Unoriginal accounts risk being demonetised. Separately, Creator Fast Track (launched 18 Mar 2026) offers three months of guaranteed pay by follower count (100,000+ followers: $1,000 a month; 1 million+: $3,000 a month) for creators new to Facebook, with US-only rules for the College Edition. The main programme's countries were not stated. It is aimed at established creators with their own audience.

**X.** Creator Revenue Sharing closed to new enrolment on 7 Aug 2026 and ends 7 Sep 2026; it is replaced by Original Content Rewards, which existing members apply for from 8 Sep 2026 (Runtime Wire 7 Aug 2026). The X Help snippet shows: 18+, at least 500 verified followers, eligible country, post original content, no paused monetisation. Premium and an impressions threshold come only from blogs. I could not open the Help page (403). Treat all X figures as unconfirmed.

**Snapchat.** Invite only. Snap Help (en-gb): 50,000 followers, 15,000 view hours in 28 days (3,000 from Spotlight), 18+, original and advertiser-friendly content, Snap Star, eligible country. Spotlight videos must be at least 30 seconds long to earn. From 7 May 2026, 100 Spotlight hours in 28 days for maximum rewards. Snap says it never charges for an invite.

**Instagram.** Bonuses are described as invite-only and time-limited. Sources disagree on pay. Not a usable route.

## 5. Service routes

- Clipping, an LA agency: pays editors $300 to $1,500 per million views; clients pay $2,500 to $10,000 a month; about $7.7 million sales in 2025 and 23,300 editors (TheWrap 27 Mar 2026; Complex 3 Nov 2025, both citing Bloomberg 28 Oct 2025).
- Lumina Clippers recommends at least $5,000 for an effective campaign; ClipFarm reportedly delivered 64 million views for $10,000 (TheWrap).
- No source I opened gives current freelance marketplace or per-clip rates for a solo clipper. Not published.
- The clean service route: agree a rate with a streamer directly, in writing.

## 6. Affiliate and sponsor routes

I found no source showing an established affiliate or sponsor route for clip accounts. Snapchat has an affiliate programme, but I did not open it and it is not about clipping. Left out.

## Rights and rules

Not legal advice.

- Permission: clips are made from someone else's stream. The clean route is the streamer's written permission or their official programme. Loeb & Loeb (22 Oct 2025) notes owners traditionally require licences and approvals. A music licence on a stream may not cover cross-posting (clipspeed, a clipping-tool blog; not authoritative).
- Copyright: Twitch removes clips and issues strikes after DMCA complaints (secondary sources). Outside the US, fair use may not apply.
- Platform rules: YouTube says permission does not make reused content eligible for monetisation. Meta and X say they reward original content. TikTok's terms put originality on the creator.
- Disclosure: Whop's terms require participants to disclose the relationship clearly beside the clip, not buried in hashtags. FTC guidance says to disclose any financial relationship, inside the video itself, and warns against vague tags such as "collab" or "ambassador". In the UK the ASA says payment includes non-cash incentives, recommends "#ad" placed first, and says a bio is not enough.
- Fraud: Whop and TikTok both exclude bought or botted views and can withhold or disqualify.

## What the app needs

- Clip length: the default is 40 s before plus 20 s after the peak, exactly 60 s. TikTok's terms say "at least 1 minute" and its newsroom says "over one minute". Set Settings > "Seconds after the peak" to 30 for a 70 s clip (40 + 30). This is my suggestion, not a TikTok figure. It matters only for Creator Rewards, which needs original content anyway, so most users will not need it. Many campaigns set their own length; follow the campaign.
- Captions: the Settings field "Starting caption ({streamer} is filled in)" is the place for campaign tags or required wording. Each clip's caption can still be edited before approval.
- Disclosure: for paid campaigns, put the required label at the start of the caption.
- Watermarks and per-post view tracking: I found neither in the web app source. Campaigns count views on their own platform, so users track views on each platform or on the campaign page.
- YouTube Shorts maximum length: not checked. Confirm before changing clip length.
